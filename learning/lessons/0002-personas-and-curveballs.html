Where Personas and Curveballs Actually Enter

One tangible win: you can point at the exact lines where a curveball gets into a generated test, and the exact mechanism that stops the LLM from inventing a persona.

The factory, with its three entry points

1 · A curveball is compiled into customer facts

Declared on one edge of the map (valid_perturbations in procedures/j1.yaml), chosen by the enumerator (every subset of what the path allows, at most two), and then — the key move — compiled into the customer by _goal_facts() in scenario_synthesis/enumerate.py:400:

if "partial_disclosure" in perturbation_types:
    facts["disclosure_style"] = "one_fact_at_a_time"   # drip-feed = a customer habit
if CARD_SWITCH_EDGE in path and len(bindings.cards) > 1:
    facts["initial_card_last_four"] = bindings.cards[0]  # switch = two bound cards
    facts["final_card_last_four"]   = bindings.cards[1]
if "submission_failure" in perturbation_types:
    facts["amount_type"] = "custom"
    facts["amount"] = LARGE_PAYMENT_THRESHOLD + 1000.0   # failure = the data itself
The insight

No curveball ever scripts the assistant. Drip-feed becomes a customer habit, card-switch becomes two bound cards, submission failure becomes an amount $1,000 over the large-payment threshold — so the mock's submit genuinely fails. The assistant's unscripted reaction is the thing being tested.

2 · A persona is picked from a menu the LLM can't escape

At realization, the persona has exactly four dimensions with two allowed values each (scenario_synthesis/persona_traits.yaml):

DimensionAllowed values
patiencepatient · mildly_impatient
attention_to_amountsattentive_to_amounts · trusts_presented_amounts
disclosure_styleconcise_upfront · answers_as_asked
decisivenessdecisive · changes_mind_once

The whitelist isn't a suggestion in the prompt — realize.py turns it into the JSON schema of the LLM's forced structured output: each dimension is an enum field. An off-menu trait isn't rejected later; it can't be produced at all. The four picked values are then joined into the trait string the simulator will actually role-play: "patient, attentive to amounts, answers as asked, changes mind once" — which is verbatim the persona of generated scenario j1-0d64354fd7908d7b, a card-switch test. Notice the coherence: a customer who switches cards is one who "changes mind once".

Two more persona layers to know about: reviewed behavioral archetypes (cooperative, pressure, vigilant, persistent) in scenario_synthesis/contracts/persona-archetypes.yaml, mapped to every hand-written scenario per ADR-0003 — and runtime overlays (persona_variants/) that append a mood without touching goal or facts.

3 · The gate that keeps the prose honest

The writer LLM is instructed: "Realize only the supplied scenario facts. Do not invent numbers, identifiers, dates, accounts, amounts, tools, or persona traits." But instruction is not enforcement — _check_equivalence() (realize.py:418) is. It regex-extracts from the generated prose:

  • every bare number, every $-amount, every date,
  • every registry tool name mentioned,

and requires each one to exist in the serialized blueprint. Any orphan fact → RealizationError, one retry with the rejection reason pasted into the prompt ("The prior output was rejected: … Correct it."), then hard failure. Creativity in wording, zero creativity in facts.
