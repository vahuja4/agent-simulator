Two Kinds of Trouble

The win for this lesson: for any tricky test, you can say whether it's a perturbation or a complication — and which one exactly.

The rule, in plain words

Every hard test makes trouble for the assistant. The repo splits trouble into two kinds:

  • A perturbation changes what happens — the data, the steps, a tool that fails.
  • A complication changes how the customer talks, or what they wrongly believe.

Example: a payment that fails because it's $6,000 — that's something that happens. A customer who says "my Freedom card" when they own two Freedom cards — that's how they talk. One chat can have both.

The repo's own words (ADR-0005)

"Procedure branches, validation outcomes, tool failures, and fixture conditions are not conversational Complications." In short: if it's about the flow or the data, it's not a complication.

The tricky cases

"My Freedom card" vs "my credit card": the first gives a clue that matches two cards (ambiguous-reference); the second gives no clue at all (underspecification).
"Make it $200" vs "forget it, do this instead": the first keeps the payment and tweaks it (correction); the second throws it away and starts over (goal-shift). What matters is keep-vs-throw-away — not how many things changed.
The trap: a customer who knowingly asks to pay $10 (below the $35 minimum) is none. Nothing is wrong with how they talk — the difficulty is in the data, so it belongs to the other kind.
